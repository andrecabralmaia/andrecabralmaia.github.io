Working Papers

Extreme Heat and Labor Market Dynamics (2026)

with Antonio Martins-Neto

Abstract

This paper investigates the effects of extreme heat on the Brazilian labor market, combining climate data with employer–employee administrative records covering the universe of formal establishments over two decades (2000–2019). We estimate a fixed-effects panel specification for short-run responses and a long-differences approach for medium-run effects. Aggregate responses to heat shocks are null and mask substantial heterogeneity across productivity levels. In the short run, additional days of extreme heat reduce employment and raise the exit probability of the least productive establishments. Plants in the middle and upper parts of the productivity distribution expand their workforce and become less likely to close – possibly due to a greater short-run adaptive capacity – which highlights a redistributive dynamic in local labor markets triggered by high temperatures. In the medium run, however, municipalities exposed to larger decennial increases in extreme heat experience a contraction in their stock of establishments. The contraction operates through entry rather than exit, and is concentrated among high-productivity plants in agriculture and manufacturing. This effect is accompanied by rising employment concentration, driven by the consolidation of high-productivity incumbents. Extreme heat thus reallocates activity toward more productive establishments in the short run, while over longer horizons this reallocation compounds into a more concentrated market structure and discourages the creation of new establishments, a result with potential implications for aggregate productivity growth in emerging economies.
